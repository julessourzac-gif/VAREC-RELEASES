Frequently asked

What people ask us

VAREC is a multitrack audio field recorder for macOS, published by BERNIK. It records up to 512 simultaneous channels from any CoreAudio interface, handles LTC and Tentacle Bluetooth timecode, writes to several disks in parallel and produces a Sound Report. It is currently in beta.

VAREC requires macOS 12 Monterey or later. It runs natively on both Apple Silicon and Intel Macs, with a separate download for each. A CoreAudio-compatible audio interface is needed, whether built in or external.

VAREC records up to 512 simultaneous channels. The number actually available depends on the connected audio interface: the application exposes every channel CoreAudio presents to it.

VAREC writes 32-bit float WAV, using the RF64 format to pass the 4 GB ceiling of a regular WAV file. The Sound Report exports to PDF, CSV and HTML.

VAREC detects timecode automatically, over LTC as well as Bluetooth through Tentacle generators. No manual setup is required: the source is recognised and the timecode is written into the files.

The files stay usable. VAREC keeps the WAV headers up to date continuously while writing, rather than writing them when the file closes, so a power cut or a crash does not leave an unreadable file. This is the Crash Recovery feature.

Yes. VAREC opens an independent write stream per disk, which is true real-time redundancy rather than a copy made after the recording. If one disk fails, the other already holds the complete take.

The download is free and asks for no email address. During the beta every feature is unlocked with no restriction. A licence will be required for commercial use; pricing will be announced later.

Windows and Linux binaries are published, but they are untested and do not track the macOS version. VAREC is developed for macOS: that is the only platform on which the application is proven.
