Privacy policy

This document sets out what personal data is processed when you visit varec.bernik.io, why, and what rights you hold. It is written pursuant to articles 12 to 14 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR). The French version prevails in case of discrepancy.

1. In short

This site asks you for nothing. No account, no form, no sign-up. Downloading VAREC is unrestricted and conditional on no email address. No cookies are set, no advertising is served, and no data is sold or shared with third parties.

What remains is the technical minimum inherent to any website: your browser sends a request to a server, which briefly records it in order to function and to defend itself against abuse. Sections 5 to 8 set out the detail.

2. Scope

This policy covers the varec.bernik.io website and nothing else.

It does not cover:

  • the VAREC application installed on your machine — its own processing (licence activation, cloud sharing features, diagnostic logs) is governed by the documentation and licence agreement supplied with it;
  • third-party sites this site links to, notably GitHub and bernik.io, which apply their own policies.

3. Data controller

Given the nature and volume of the processing described below, no data protection officer has been appointed: the conditions for mandatory appointment under article 37 GDPR are not met. The address above is the single point of contact.

4. No direct collection

The site has no form, no input field and no user account. You are never asked for an email address, a name, or any other information about you.

In particular, downloading VAREC is conditional on nothing. An earlier mechanism required an email address before unlocking the download; it has been removed and the corresponding data deleted (see section 16).

If you write to us at contact@bernik.io of your own accord, your message and address are processed in order to reply — that is the only case in which data reaches us through an act of yours.

6. Audience measurement

Some pages use Vercel Web Analytics, the measurement tool built into our host. It has three characteristics, which is why you are shown no consent banner:

  • it sets no cookie and writes nothing to your browser;
  • it allows no cross-site tracking: measurement stops at the boundaries of varec.bernik.io;
  • it does not retain your IP address: the address is used to derive a non-reversible visit identifier, renewed daily, which distinguishes two visits without ever identifying you.

Configured this way, the measurement falls within the consent exemption the French supervisory authority (CNIL) grants to audience measurement tools strictly limited to producing anonymous statistics for the publisher alone.

Opting out. You can object by enabling your browser's "Do Not Track" setting, which is honoured, or by blocking the cdn.vercel-insights.com domain with a content blocker. The site remains fully functional either way.

7. Web fonts

The site displays the Inter typeface, loaded from Google's servers (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com). That load sends Google your IP address and the technical information your browser supplies, in the United States.

We flag this explicitly because the point is contested: several European authorities and courts take the view that such loading should require the visitor's consent, or be avoided altogether. Removing this dependency — by hosting the font on the site itself — is under consideration.

In the meantime, blocking those two domains does not prevent you from reading the site: the layout simply falls back to a system typeface.

8. Cookies and local storage

The site sets no cookies — none for measurement, advertising or personalisation. That is why no banner is shown: there is nothing to accept or refuse.

The site does use one sessionStorage entry, named varec_releases, which caches the list of published versions for the duration of your visit so that GitHub's API need not be queried on every page. This storage:

  • contains no data about you — only version numbers and public download links;
  • never leaves your browser;
  • disappears when you close the tab.

Being strictly necessary to provide the service you requested, it falls within the consent exemption of article 82 of the French Data Protection Act.

12. Your rights

Under articles 15 to 22 GDPR you hold the following rights:

  • Access — confirmation as to whether data about you is processed, and a copy of it;
  • Rectification — correction of inaccurate data;
  • Erasure — deletion of your data, subject to legal retention obligations;
  • Restriction — suspension of processing while a challenge is examined;
  • Objection — objection, on grounds relating to your particular situation, to processing based on legitimate interests;
  • Portability — receipt in a structured format of the data you provided, where processing rests on consent or a contract;
  • Post-mortem directives — instructions as to what becomes of your data after your death, under article 85 of the French Data Protection Act.

To exercise these rights, write to contact@bernik.io. You will receive a reply within one month of receipt of your request, extendable by two months where its complexity warrants — in which case you will be told.

Please note. Because the site keeps no identifier linking you to a visit, an access request concerning your browsing will most often return nothing: there is nothing to disclose. That is the direct consequence of collecting nothing, not a refusal.

14. Security

The site is served exclusively over HTTPS. It is static: it runs no server-side code and holds no database, which reduces the attack surface to a minimum. Installer files are distributed through GitHub Releases and signed in accordance with Apple's notarisation requirements.

15. Minors

VAREC is aimed at sound professionals. The site is not directed at children under fifteen and knowingly collects no data concerning them.

16. History — the collection mechanism that was removed

For the sake of transparency, and because people may have supplied their address under it:

Until 4 September 2026, downloading VAREC was preceded by a dialog requiring an email address, which was recorded together with the date, version and architecture downloaded.

That mechanism did not comply with the GDPR: making access to a free download conditional on data that was not necessary for that service deprived consent of its freely-given character, within the meaning of article 7(4) and recital 43. The information provided at the point of collection was also inadequate.

It has been removed in full: the dialog, the collection endpoint and the storage file are gone, and the recorded addresses have been deleted. No marketing will be sent on the basis of that list.

If you supplied your address this way and would like confirmation of its deletion, write to contact@bernik.io.

This policy may change, in particular if new site features come to involve new processing. The date of the last update appears below; any substantial change will be signalled on the home page.
